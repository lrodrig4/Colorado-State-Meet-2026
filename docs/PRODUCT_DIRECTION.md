# Colorado Distance Intel Product Direction

## Thesis

Colorado Distance Intel should become a coach-first XC and distance intelligence product. MileSplit and Athletic.net can remain the raw-results systems of record; this product should win by interpreting results for coaches, runners, and fans.

## Wedge

- Start in Colorado, where the current state-qualification engine already has useful data and coach workflows.
- Keep the command center as the core paid value: saved team, rankings, qualification odds, projected scoring, depth charts, weekend decisions, and shareable graphics.
- Build a public content layer around the engine: previews, rankings context, notebooks, charts, photos, and personality-driven columns.

## Revenue Path

- Early offer: founder coach access for individual coaches.
- Program offer: team season subscription priced like a coach tool, not a consumer blog.
- Later revenue: sponsored ranking graphics, meet previews, newsletter placements, and local running-business ads.

## Product Phases

1. Public positioning: branded navigation, `Distance Intel` content hub, and `Coach Pro` subscription positioning.
2. Lead capture: founder coach interest form with school, classification, email, and requested features.
3. Private access: auth-protected coach dashboards and team-specific saved views.
4. XC expansion: course context, team scoring, regional/state projections, and weekly watchlists.
5. Media system: article publishing, image/card generation, newsletter, and sponsor inventory.

## Guardrails

- Do not compete on raw results ownership.
- Do not build billing before there is coach demand.
- Keep the first paid promise narrow: make a coach better prepared for the next important race.
- Public content should always point back to a practical coach workflow.
- Position the MVP honestly: track state-week command center now, XC intelligence next.
